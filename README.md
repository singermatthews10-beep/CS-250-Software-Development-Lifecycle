# CS 250: Software Development Lifecycle

## Sprint Review and Retrospective

This repository contains my Sprint Review and Retrospective from CS 250: Software Development Lifecycle at Southern New Hampshire University. The project demonstrates my understanding of Agile and Scrum practices, including gathering requirements, developing user stories, collaborating with team members, adapting to changes, and reflecting on the software development process.

Throughout this course, I explored different Scrum roles and learned how communication, teamwork, and continuous improvement contribute to successful software development.

## How Do I Interpret User Needs and Implement Them Into a Program? How Does Creating User Stories Help With This?

I interpret user needs by first understanding what the user wants to accomplish and identifying the features that will help meet those expectations. It is important to ask questions, gather requirements, and communicate with stakeholders before beginning development. Creating user stories helps break down larger requirements into smaller, manageable tasks from the user's perspective. During the SNHU Travel project, I learned how user stories help developers understand the purpose of a feature and how it should function. User stories also provide a way to prioritize requirements and establish acceptance criteria to determine whether a feature meets the user's expectations.

## How Do I Approach Developing Programs? What Agile Processes Do I Hope to Incorporate Into My Future Development Work?

When developing programs, I believe it is important to begin with a clear understanding of the requirements, create a plan, and break the project into manageable tasks. Throughout CS 250, I learned that Agile development provides flexibility by allowing teams to develop software in smaller increments and continuously improve their work. In my future software development career, I hope to incorporate sprint planning, daily Scrum meetings, sprint reviews, and retrospectives. I also want to use tools such as Scrum boards and product backlogs to track progress and manage priorities. These practices will help me stay organized, respond to changing requirements, and deliver software that meets user needs.

## What Does It Mean to Be a Good Team Member in Software Development?

Being a good team member in software development means communicating effectively, being dependable, respecting different perspectives, and working toward a shared goal. Throughout this course, I learned that successful Agile teams depend on collaboration between developers, Scrum Masters, Product Owners, and stakeholders. Each team member has responsibilities that contribute to the success of the project. I also learned the importance of being open to constructive feedback, helping teammates overcome challenges, and taking responsibility for my work. As I continue preparing for a career in software engineering, I want to be someone my team can rely on and someone who contributes to a positive and productive development environment.
